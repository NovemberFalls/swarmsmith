# SEALED INTENT — what the caller actually wants. Neither arm may see this.

Opening prompt, verbatim and complete: "lets make a browser based mmorpg"

## The truth behind those six words

1. It is for **six named friends**, not the public. They are a tabletop group that has
   played together for eleven years and can no longer meet in person since two of them
   moved away. There is no ambition to launch, monetise, or grow. Strangers must not be
   able to wander in.
2. **The point is being in the same place at the same time.** What they miss is the
   hanging around — the talking, the messing about between the interesting bits. If the
   world is empty when you log in, it has failed, no matter what else works.
3. **Combat is not the product.** They would take a world with no combat at all over a
   world with good combat and nobody in it. Combat is somewhere below chat, presence, and
   a shared persistent space in the ordering.
4. **Half of them will be on a phone**, on the sofa, in short bursts. If it needs a
   keyboard it excludes half the group.
5. **The caller cannot make art.** No sprites, no tilesets, no commissioning. Whatever it
   looks like has to come from shapes, text, or something free — this is a hard limit and
   not a preference.
6. **It must survive being empty.** Six people in five time zones are rarely online
   together, so the world has to be worth visiting alone and has to show what happened
   while you were away.
7. **It runs on one small VPS the caller already pays for**, alongside two other things.
   Nothing that needs a fleet, a game server per zone, or a GPU.
8. **The caller has built exactly one thing before**: a Discord bot, in Python, that ran
   for a year. They are not a game developer and have never written a game loop.
9. **Persistence matters more than scale.** Ten concurrent players is the ceiling
   forever. Losing a character would end the project socially — these are people's
   characters from an eleven-year campaign.
10. **The first thing worth showing anyone** is two friends standing in the same room,
    seeing each other move, and talking. Not a combat demo. Not a character creator.

## Caller behaviour, if interrogated

- Confident about their friends, their group's history, and what they miss. Vague about
  everything technical: "I don't know", "whatever you think". Never names a technology.
- **Never volunteers any of the ten items above.** Answers honestly and in detail when
  asked directly. If asked something not covered here, says so in character and does NOT
  invent new facts.
- Never authorises plan writing; confirms only an explicit closed question.
